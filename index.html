Intro

ART (Automated Resilience Testing) is a diagnostic tool for teachers. It takes a Moodle course — a live instance or a .mbz backup restored into one — and drives an AI through every quiz question exactly as a student would, then produces a per-question report showing how Moodle graded each one: correct, wrong, partial, or awaiting manual grading.

Every number on the screen comes from Moodle's own grading, or is explicitly labelled "not verified". No fabricated scores, no invented success badges — check out the details.

Work

  • For each question in a course, drives an LLM to answer it the way a student would.
  • Submits the AI's answers to Moodle exactly as a student would.
  • Reports Moodle's per-question grading (correct / partial / wrong / needs manual review) verbatim, per question.
  • Shows the teacher a course-level capability audit: quiz count, per-type question breakdown, unsupported types, empty quizzes, uninspected quizzes.

Question-type aware: MCQ, True/False, Short Answer, Essay, Matching, Numerical, Calculated (3 variants), Drag Words into Text Sentences, Gap Select, Drag and Drop Marker, Drag Image or Text, Cloze, Ordering, Random Short Answer Matching. Unknown types fall back to the essay handler with an explicit warning and an unsupported flag in the audit.

Ships with a GUI installer that automates the full local Moodle environment (Windows-first), .mbz course import, LTI 1.3 tool auto-registration, and a debug console with real-time tree-view logging.

About

ART is delivered as a working diagnostic tool demonstrated on real Moodle courses, plus a lõputöö (thesis) at Tallinna Polütehnikum that describes the design and its rationale.

Design principle: truthfulness first. The tool never claims correctness unless Moodle's per-slot grading returned that verdict, never labels an answer "successful" just because the payload was delivered, and never invents a "% AI-resilient" score. Essays awaiting manual review are shown as "Awaiting manual grading" — not as a fake score.

What ART does not do: rewrite questions, generate replacement quizzes, grade students, tutor, or take grading authority away from the teacher.
